The issues shaping West Palm Beach.

Downtown Master Plan

Long-range decisions that will shape downtown and the waterfront for decades — including building heights, density, and the public review process.

Take action

Sign: Protect the Palm Sign: Save WPB

Why it matters

The Downtown Master Plan (DMP) sets the rules for height, density, and land use across the city core. Proposed changes could add over 1,200 high-rise units along the waterfront corridor, with towers ranging from roughly 18 to 31 stories. Residents are asking for adequate time to review mobility and infrastructure impacts before the plan is finalized.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Allow adequate time for mobility and infrastructure questions to be answered before the DMP is finalized; support meaningful resident participation in the process.

Key dates

  • Apr 25, 2024 — Comprehensive Plan / DMP element adopted (Ord. 5071-23)
  • 2025–2026 — DMP update process underway at the Planning Division
  • Verify upcoming Downtown Action Committee (DAC) and Commission dates against the City calendar

Upcoming meetings

  • Downtown Action Committee (DAC) — see Calendar for the next public meeting
  • City Commission hearings on DMP amendments — dates to be confirmed

Relevant studies

  • City of WPB — Downtown Master Plan (official page)
  • Downtown WPB Mobility Plan (2018)

Plan of action

  • Track the public process and post every hearing date on the Calendar
  • Encourage residents to attend DAC and Commission meetings and submit comment
  • Coordinate an independent review of traffic and infrastructure impacts

Traffic & Mobility

How new growth affects congestion, parking, transit, and pedestrian and cyclist safety across the city.

Why it matters

Each major project adds trips to roads that were not designed for this pace of growth. Residents want long-term planning aligned with the County Transportation Master Plan and regional mobility studies, not project-by-project review.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Align long-term city planning with the County Transportation Master Plan and regional mobility studies; fund independent traffic and mobility analysis.

Key dates

  • Verify against the County Transportation Planning Agency (TPA) meeting calendar

Upcoming meetings

  • Palm Beach TPA board meetings — add dates to the Calendar

Relevant studies

  • Downtown WPB Mobility Plan (2018)

Plan of action

  • Commission or compile independent traffic and mobility analysis
  • Map cumulative trip impacts from the development pipeline
  • Advocate for a coordinated, citywide mobility approach
Watching

Infrastructure Capacity

Whether water, sewer, drainage, and stormwater systems can support the scale of approved and proposed growth.

Why it matters

Much of the city's infrastructure is aging. Rapid high-density growth raises questions about capacity for water, sewer, drainage, and resilience that residents want answered before, not after, approvals.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Infrastructure capacity and resilience should be evaluated and disclosed before major approvals.

Key dates

  • Tie to City budget and Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) cycles — verify dates

Upcoming meetings

  • City Commission budget / CIP workshops — add to Calendar

Relevant studies

  • City of WPB — Public Utilities

Plan of action

  • Request capacity and CIP documentation through public records
  • Summarize findings in plain language for residents
  • Track infrastructure commitments tied to each major project
Watching

Public Safety

Police, fire-rescue, and neighborhood safety keeping pace with population growth.

Why it matters

A growing residential downtown and denser neighborhoods change demands on police and fire-rescue. Residents want service levels and response times to keep pace with growth.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Public-safety staffing and response capacity should scale with new development.

Key dates

  • Tie to City budget cycle — verify dates

Upcoming meetings

  • Neighborhood public-safety briefings — add to Calendar

Relevant studies

  • City of WPB — Police Department

Plan of action

  • Track public-safety staffing and budget against growth
  • Coordinate neighborhood safety concerns citywide

Waterfront Planning

Protecting public access, green space, views, and the Intracoastal ecosystem along the waterfront.

Take action

Sign: Protect the Palm

Why it matters

The waterfront is a shared public asset. Proposed towers along the corridor raise concerns about shadows on historic neighborhoods, public access, and the health of the Intracoastal ecosystem, seagrass, and manatees.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Protect public waterfront access, green space, and environmental resilience for the long term.

Key dates

  • Verify against DAC and Commission waterfront agenda items

Upcoming meetings

  • Waterfront-related Commission and DAC hearings — add to Calendar

Relevant studies

  • City of WPB — Waterfront & Parks

Plan of action

  • Track waterfront projects on the Development Tracker
  • Advocate for public access and environmental review
  • Support resident participation in waterfront decisions

Development Pipeline

The full slate of major projects proposed, approved, and under construction across the city.

Why it matters

Dozens of major projects are moving through the pipeline from Northwood to SoSo. Looking at them together — not one at a time — is the only way to understand the cumulative impact on traffic, infrastructure, and neighborhood character.

Coalition position

Draft — pending Board confirmation: Evaluate growth cumulatively and transparently so it strengthens neighborhoods.

Key dates

  • Updated continuously — see the Development Tracker

Upcoming meetings

  • Planning Board and DAC project hearings — add to Calendar

Relevant studies

  • WPBRC Development Tracker

Plan of action

  • Maintain the interactive Development Tracker
  • Publish a plain-language summary of cumulative impacts
  • Flag projects with citywide significance for resident attention

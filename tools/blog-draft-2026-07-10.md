# Downtown Master Plan votes postponed to fall after resident input

**Date:** July 10, 2026 · **Author:** WPBRC · **Tag:** Update

*Mayor Keith James has paused the Downtown Master Plan update — including votes that were weeks away — promising a more transparent process this fall. Here's what happened and what it means for residents.*

---

The biggest development story of the week isn't a building — it's a pause. On July 7, Mayor Keith James announced that votes on the Downtown Master Plan update are postponed until fall, following sustained input from residents and neighborhood organizations, including this Coalition and the Downtown Neighborhood Association.

## What was on the table

The proposed update would reshape downtown development rules: a five-story baseline height with bonuses allowing towers up to 25 stories along Flagler Drive, reduced parking requirements, a western innovation district, and larger waterfront setbacks. A key city committee had been scheduled to take up the plan in early July, with a City Commission vote tentatively set for August 31. Those votes are now off the calendar until fall.

## What the city committed to

The mayor said the city will create a more inclusive and transparent process, including giving residents direct access to city staff and technical experts before decisions are made. "Our downtown belongs to everyone, and its future should be shaped through meaningful collaboration," he said in a statement. We will post new hearing dates on our events page as soon as the city publishes them.

## Also this week

The Berkeley Palm Beach broke ground July 6 — a 25-story condominium on South Australian Avenue overlooking Clear Lake, already tracked as Under Construction on our development tracker. In Northwood, sales are underway for Nora House, an 11-story, roughly 117-residence condo planned as the NORA District's first for-sale building, with groundbreaking anticipated in 2027. And on July 6, the City Commission voted 4-1 to appoint a Related Ross executive to the Downtown Development Authority board, a decision some residents questioned given the firm's extensive downtown holdings.

## Why it matters

The postponement means residents have more time — and a promised seat at the table — to weigh in on rules that will shape downtown for decades. The Coalition's position is about process, not personalities: decisions of this scale should be transparent, well-noticed, and informed by the people who live here. Watch the events page for the rescheduled hearings this fall.

# The South Flagler pause is now in effect — what it does (and doesn't) do

**Date:** July 24, 2026 · **Author:** WPBRC · **Tag:** Update

*The City Commission unanimously adopted the six-month South Flagler "zoning in progress" on Monday. Here's what the pause actually covers, what it leaves untouched, and what else passed the same night.*

---

The vote we previewed last week is done. On Monday, July 20, the City Commission unanimously adopted Ordinance No. 5177-26 on second reading, putting a six-month "zoning in progress" in place for the South Flagler corridor. The pause blocks new planned development applications on seven older multifamily-zoned properties between Monroe Drive and Southern Boulevard, from Flagler Drive to Washington Road, and runs through January 20, 2027 unless extended.

## Not quite a moratorium

City staff drew a distinction worth understanding. Several speakers called the measure a moratorium, but Assistant Development Services Director Angela Jones Vaughn told the Commission it does not stop redevelopment: a project that meets today's code requirements can still be built by right. What's paused is the filing of new *planned development* applications — the route developers use to seek towers beyond what base zoning allows. During the pause, consultant Zyscovich will run a zoning and economic analysis while city staff handles community outreach, with proposed overlay regulations targeted for January 2027 — a process modeled on the one completed for the North Flagler corridor last year.

## Residents on both sides

Public comment was split. Owners at older buildings like the Flagler Yacht Club described state-mandated structural assessments running into the millions and said sales to developers may be their only realistic exit. Other neighbors urged the Commission to hold the line and said they've already begun organizing to shape the overlay rules. Both concerns are real, and both will be on the table as the corridor study moves forward.

## Also passed Monday

The same meeting approved the Alton West Palm Beach project — 318 rental units at 5710 North Haverhill Road, 44 of them affordable or workforce — and an affordable housing covenant reserving 71 of District Pointe's 280 units on Belvedere Road for households at or below 120% of area median income.

## Elsewhere this week

Alida Residences broke ground next to the Brightline station downtown: 21 stories with 181 rentals and a 112-key Marriott Tribute hotel, expected in 2028. And Alba Palm Beach at 4714 N. Flagler delivered, reported 95% sold. Both updates are queued for our [development tracker](development.html), and meeting dates are on our [events page](events.html).

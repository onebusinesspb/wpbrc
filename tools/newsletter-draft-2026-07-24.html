Development & Meetings Update · July 24, 2026

Hi neighbor,

The vote we flagged last week is done: on Monday the City Commission unanimously adopted the six-month pause on new planned development applications along South Flagler. Here's your short, non-partisan read on what it actually does — and everything else that moved this week.

Development watch

  • South Flagler pause adopted — with a caveat. Ordinance 5177-26 passed second reading July 20. It blocks new planned development applications on seven older multifamily properties between Monroe Drive and Southern Boulevard through January 20, 2027. City staff stressed it is not a full moratorium: projects that meet today's code can still build by right. Consultant Zyscovich will study the corridor, with new overlay zoning rules targeted for January 2027. Public comment was split — some condo owners facing multi-million-dollar structural assessments opposed the pause; other neighbors backed it and are organizing input for the overlay process.
  • Alton West Palm Beach approved. The same meeting cleared 318 rental units at 5710 N. Haverhill Road (44 affordable/workforce), plus an affordable-housing covenant reserving 71 of District Pointe's 280 units on Belvedere Road.
  • Alida Residences broke ground downtown. A 21-story tower next to the Brightline station: 181 luxury rentals plus a 112-key Marriott Tribute hotel, expected in 2028.
  • Milestones on North Flagler. Alba Palm Beach (4714 N. Flagler, 22 stories) delivered at a reported 95% sold, and The Berkeley (550 S. Australian) officially broke ground with completion targeted for 2029. Both are queued for tracker updates.
